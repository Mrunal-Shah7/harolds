# Sprint 19: Apple Pay and Google Pay

Wallets are two more ways to produce the same `payment_token`. The sale request, the idempotency
guard, the response mapping, the classification and the records are the ones cards already use.
Both wallets are **off by default** behind server-side flags.

### 0.4 How the total becomes final

- **The server computes every figure.** `/api/v1/quote` → `quoteCart` (`@harolds/pricing`). The
  checkout page holds `quote.totalCents` (`page.tsx:88-104`).
- **The tip** is chosen on the checkout page (`page.tsx:488-563`). A preset commits on click. A
  custom amount commits **on every keystroke** that validates (l.535-545). Each change re-quotes:
  the effect at l.106-110 depends on `[lines.length, tip]`.
- **Finding: a quantity change does not re-quote.** The cart sheet is reachable from checkout
  ("Back to cart", l.303-310). Changing a quantity there keeps `lines.length` the same, so the page
  shows a stale total until something else re-quotes. For cards this is display-only, because the
  sale is charged at the server's own total (`checkout.ts:414-427` re-prices, and `:731` charges
  `order.totalCents`). For a wallet it would put a stale amount on the sheet. Sprint 19 re-quotes on
  any change to the cart request (§4).
- The total can therefore change after the customer starts paying through a tip edit, a cart edit,
  or a server-side price, tax or tip-preset change between the quote and the order.

### 0.5 The validation and submission sequence

1. Client, every render: `validateCheckout` (`page.tsx:149`, `lib/checkout-validation.ts:112-130`)
   checks names, phone, email, custom tip, note and ZIP. `canSubmitForm = formValid && quote.orderable` (l.151).
2. Pay click (`handlePayClick`, l.240-267): it marks every field touched, stops if not submittable,
   runs the decoy and 3 s minimum-fill checks, and then calls `requestTokenize()`.
3. Collect.js validates the card iframes and calls `callback` with the token → `submitOrder` (l.158-232).
4. Server, `checkoutOrder` (`lib/checkout.ts:357-531`):
   - `parseCreateOrderBody` (l.166-352) rejects client money fields and checks the idempotency key,
     the token, the customer, the phone, the email, the ZIP and the cart.
   - It replays by idempotency key.
   - It re-quotes, with availability, store open and accepting.
   - It creates the order through the duplicate guard (Sprint 16).
   - It charges through `chargeExistingPending` → claim → `chargeClaimedOrder` → `createPayment`.

Validation runs **before** tokenisation, and the order is created **after** it.

## 3. Tabs and the hidden-container finding (Phase 3)

**Phase 0.10, measured.** Headless Chrome ran against the generic NMI sandbox `Collect.js`: rendering only, no
`startPaymentRequest`, no tokenise.

| Container while configure() ran | Card iframes while hidden | After shown | Google Pay iframe |
|---|---|---|---|
| visible | 300×46 | 300×46 | 300×45 |
| `display: none` | **0×0** | 300×46 | 0×0, then 300×45 |
| `visibility: hidden; height: 0; position: absolute` | 300×46 | 300×46 | 300×45 |

`display: none` initialised in Chrome and recovered once shown, but until then the frames are laid out at zero size.
Apple's button cannot be tested headlessly: Chrome has no Apple Pay, and `canMakePayments()` throws on an insecure origin.

**Approach:** every panel stays mounted. An inactive panel is `visibility: hidden` with zero height
(`.pm-panel-hidden`), plus `inert`. It keeps its width, is invisible, takes no space, and is out of the tab order and the
accessibility tree. This is safe whichever way the Apple button behaves.

- **Tabs.** The component is `components/storefront/payment-method-tabs.tsx`. The rules live in `lib/payment-methods.ts`,
  which is pure and tested. Card is first and is the default.
  - **With both flags off, the page renders the pre-sprint markup:** `cardFields` and `billingZipField`, with no wrappers
    and no strip. Collect.js gets the pre-sprint configure object, `price: "1.00"`, configured once.
  - With a flag on but no wallet available, there is also no strip. The wallet panels are still mounted but hidden, so
    Collect.js can draw into them and availability can be read.
- **An unavailable wallet is hidden, not disabled.** A disabled "Apple Pay" on Android advertises a method the device
  cannot use, and adds a control that does nothing.
- **Availability.** "Not known yet" counts as no.
  - **Apple:** `ApplePaySession.canMakePayments()` **and** Collect.js having drawn its button. Collect.js only draws it
    when that same call returns true.
  - **Google:** Collect.js gives no signal; its iframe only ever posts `google-pay-field-completed`. So the check is
    Google's `PaymentsClient.isReadyToPay` **and** the iframe being mounted.
    - pay.js is loaded **only** when the flag is on.
    - The environment is TEST or PRODUCTION, following `NMI_ENVIRONMENT`.
- **ZIP.** It lives in the Card panel.
  - `validateCheckout(…, { requireBillingZip })` gained an optional argument that defaults to required, so every existing
    caller and test is unchanged.
  - On a wallet tab the ZIP is neither required nor sent.
  - A typed ZIP stays in state in case the customer returns to Card.
  - The server drops any ZIP that arrives with a wallet token.
- **Pickup details** stay outside the tabs and stay required for every method.
- **The gate (Critical rule 5).** The wallet button can't be reached until `walletSheetBlockers` returns nothing.
  - While the gate is closed, the mount is `inert`, so no click, tap or key reaches it. A transparent `<button>` sits over it.
  - Pressing it marks the fields touched, which shows the card path's own messages, and a `help` line says why.
  - It blocks on:
    - an invalid field (the ZIP excepted);
    - an order that isn't orderable;
    - a quote that is loading, missing, or **not the price Collect.js holds**;
    - a submission in progress, or a sheet already open;
    - the 18.x retry lockout;
    - the decoy or the 3 s minimum-fill check.
- **Accessibility:** `tablist`/`tab`/`tabpanel` roles; a roving tabindex; arrows (wrapping), Home and End; the global blue
  focus ring; and a polite "Paying with …" live region.
- **Mobile.** Measured in headless Chrome with the real Baloo 2 font and the `globals.css` rules:
  - at **360px**, each tab is 90px wide and 44px tall, with `scrollWidth == clientWidth`, so there is no overflow and no
    truncation;
  - at 320px, each tab is 77px wide, still with no overflow.
- **Design files.**
  - `design.md` gained a "Payment-method tabs (Sprint 19)" section.
  - `harolds-design-v1_1.html` mirrors the CSS block verbatim, and the markup for the wallets-on state with Card selected.
    It also now shows the 18.3 billing ZIP, which it lacked.

## 4. Amount integrity (Phase 4)

- **The price never exists in the browser as arithmetic.**
  - `/api/v1/quote` adds `totalGatewayAmount = toGatewayAmount(totalCents)`. This is the **same formatter** the sale
    uses for `amount` (`@harolds/payments`, now exported).
  - The page passes it through `walletPriceFromQuote` (an identity function) to `collectJsWalletOptions(flags, price)`,
    then to `CollectJS.configure({ price })`.
  - Tests check:
    - identity on strings no arithmetic would reproduce ("0007.10", "12.345");
    - a source scan of the code that carries the price;
    - that a tip change produces a new quote, a new configured price, and a sheet blocked while the old price is held.
- **Price updates: re-configure.** Collect.js has no price-update call.
  - The source does have an `updateAmount`, but it updates the **surcharge** input, not the wallet price.
  - The Google Pay iframe takes `price` in its URL at configure time.
  - So `configure()` re-runs, debounced 400 ms, when the server price changes **while a wallet tab is selected**.
  - It does not re-run on the Card tab, because a redraw clears a half-typed card. It runs when a wallet tab is chosen.
  - The gate blocks until Collect.js holds the current price, so **the sheet cannot show a stale total**.
- **Re-quote on any cart change.** The page used to re-quote on `[lines.length, tip]` only (the Phase 0.4 finding).
  - It now keys on the full cart request.
  - A sequence guard stops an older quote response from overwriting a newer one.
  - This also fixes the card path's stale *display* after a quantity change in the cart sheet. That is a deviation: it
    changes what the card path displays, not what it charges.
- **While a sheet is open,** the tip buttons, the custom tip, "Back to cart", "Keep shopping" and the tabs are disabled.
  - Opening is detected from a press on Apple's in-page button, or from the window losing focus to Google's iframe.
  - Closing is detected from the callback, from focus returning, or from a touch outside the button.
  - This is best-effort, because Collect.js reports neither opening nor cancelling. The server check is the guarantee.
- **Enforcement.** Neither NMI nor MPC documents the gateway holding a wallet sale to the authorised amount, **so the
  server enforces it.**
  - `walletAmountRefusal` runs in `chargeClaimedOrder` immediately before the one `createPayment`. A new order, a key
    replay, a duplicate-guard hit and a recovered claim all pass through it.
  - It compares `walletDisplayedAmount` with `toGatewayAmount(order.totalCents)` byte for byte.
  - On a mismatch it:
    - releases the claim;
    - returns **500 `INTERNAL_ERROR`** with `details.reason: WALLET_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` and `retryable: true`;
    - tells the customer: "Your order total changed before we could charge it, so nothing was charged. Please check the
      new total and pay again.";
    - logs `checkout.wallet_amount_mismatch`.
  - **The gateway is never called on a mismatch.** A test asserts the fetch count is 0.
  - The storefront re-quotes after the refusal.
- **Deviation: the field name.** `walletDisplayedAmount` deliberately passes the top-level money guard
  (`/(Cents|Price)$/`, `amount`, `total`, …).
  - It is not a price. It is only compared, and the server charges its own total.
  - The guard itself is unchanged. A test shows `amount` and `walletPrice` are still refused.

## 5. Charge path and records (Phase 5)

- **One charge path.** `wallet-checkout.test.ts` scans every non-test source file:
  - `type: "sale"` appears only in `packages/payments/src/client.ts`;
  - `createPayment({` appears only in `apps/web/src/lib/checkout.ts`;
  - `applepay_payment_data`, `googlepay_payment_data` and `decrypted_*` appear nowhere.

  A wallet sale's request equals the card's field for field, minus `zip`. The token rides in `payment_token`.
- **Postal code for AVS.** Each wallet is asked for the minimum:
  - Apple: `postalAddress`, since there is no ZIP-only field.
  - Google: `billingAddressRequired` with `MIN`.

  The docs say the gateway takes the postal code from the token, so **no `zip` is sent for a wallet**. It is not
  persisted and not logged; the storefront never reads `response.wallet`.
- **Migration `20260926120000_sprint19_payment_method`.**
  - Generated with `prisma migrate diff` against the database at 18.3, and applied with `migrate deploy`.
  - It has 0 CR bytes. The rollback is in `prisma/rollbacks/`.

  ```sql
  CREATE TYPE "PaymentMethod" AS ENUM ('card', 'apple_pay', 'google_pay');
  ALTER TABLE "PaymentAttempt" ADD COLUMN "cardBrand" TEXT,
  ADD COLUMN "paymentMethod" "PaymentMethod" NOT NULL DEFAULT 'card';
  ```

  - The columns are on `PaymentAttempt`, the 18.3 per-attempt record that admin shows beside AVS and CVV. The admin table
    gains a **Method** column (for example "Apple Pay · visa").
  - Card brand is `card.type` from the callback, normalised to lowercase letters. It is diagnostic only.
  - **The masked number was not added.** The callback's `card.number` is `null` for wallets, and `wallet.cardDetails`
    is client-reported. The gateway-reported last 4 still lands in `Order.cardLast4`, as before.
- **A wallet payment's persisted record** (from the test run, redacted):

  ```
  { paymentMethod: "apple_pay", cardBrand: "visa", classification: "APPROVED",
    internalReason: "APPROVED", gatewayResponseCode: "100", avsResponse: "Z",
    cvvResponse: null, authCode: "654321", gatewayTransactionId: "1255591…",
    amountCents: 1840, gatewayEnvironment: "sandbox" }
  ```

  There is no token, ZIP, cryptogram or wallet contact field in it. Every table was scanned for each such string before
  and after the charge.
- **Idempotency.** The same Google Pay token was submitted twice concurrently, through the Sprint 16 guard and the
  Sprint 17 claim, against a 50 ms stubbed gateway. The result was **one order, one sale POST and one attempt row**.
  Submitting it again sequentially is a replay, not a sale.
- **Classification.** Card, Apple Pay and Google Pay each got the same stubbed gateway answers:
  - `201` gives 402 `PAYMENT_DECLINED`, and the result object is **identical across the three methods**.
  - `411` gives 503 `PAYMENT_UNAVAILABLE`, releases the claim, and raises **exactly one** alert per method.
- **Void and refund are unchanged.** Both act on the gateway `transactionid`, whatever produced the token. Source: MPC
  export §Void (l.56, l.323) and §Refund (l.62). The admin actions use `Order.processorPaymentId`.
- **The log line.** `payment.outcome` gains `paymentMethod` and nothing else. `payment.attempt` is unchanged.

## 8. Deviations

1. **`mv` + `git add` instead of `git mv`** for the association file, which was untracked (§1).
2. **The association file has no `SPRINT-19` header** because it is byte-sensitive. `design.md` carries its marker in
   the new section rather than on line 1, because YAML frontmatter must come first.
3. **The MPC portal was not read.** Rule 2 forbids contacting the MPC host. The local MPC Payment API export and NMI's
   public docs were used, and **every Collect.js wallet fact rests on NMI alone** (§0).
4. **Collect.js's source was read** (the sandbox `https://sandbox.nmi.com/token/Collect.js`, a generic NMI host). It
   settled four things the docs leave open:
   - the wallet iframe host (`collectcheckout.com`);
   - that there is no Google Pay readiness signal;
   - that `updateAmount` is the surcharge input;
   - that `timeoutCallback` also fires for wallet-token failures.
5. **A CSP origin that is not in NMI's list:** `https://collectcheckout.com` in `frame-src`, while Google Pay is on.
6. **Google's pay.js is loaded when Google Pay is on**, only to ask `isReadyToPay`. Collect.js reports no Google Pay
   availability, and the brief requires one.
7. **Two 18.2 config tests adjusted** (§2). No card-checkout test was edited.
8. **Re-quoting on any cart change** also corrects the card path's stale *displayed* total after a quantity change.
   Nothing the card path charges changes.
9. **The amount refusal is a 500 `INTERNAL_ERROR`** with `details.reason: WALLET_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` and
   `retryable: true`, per the brief ("an internal error that tells the customer to retry"). It is logged, not recorded
   as a `PaymentAttempt`, because no gateway attempt happened.
10. **A ZIP sent with a wallet token is dropped, not refused.** Refusing it after the customer has approved the sheet
    would punish a client bug on the customer. It is never sent to the gateway.
11. **A wallet whose flag is off is refused with 400** before any order exists: the kill switch covers a page rendered
    before the restart.
12. **Card brand** is recorded for cards too, taken from the same callback field. **The masked number was not added**
    (§5).
13. **The ZIP stays typed across tab switches, but typed card digits do not survive a switch to a wallet tab.** The
    switch re-draws Collect.js with the current price, which clears the card fields. This is recorded in
    OUTSTANDING.
